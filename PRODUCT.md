# Product

## Register

product

## Users

The owner of this machine, working from a Windows laptop tethered to a phone hotspot on a metered carrier plan. They open the app several times a day, usually mid-task and mildly anxious about the monthly cap. The job: know in two seconds how many GB are left and whether today's pace is safe, then get back to work. Secondary jobs: calibrate against the carrier's app, see which app is eating data, adjust plan settings.

## Product Purpose

DataGuard counts every byte this laptop uses toward the carrier plan, warns before the plan runs out, and can cut the internet when the daily hotspot cap is hit. It exists because carrier apps are slow, vague and reactive: this tool is exact, local and early. Success: the owner never gets surprised by the cap and only opens the carrier app to calibrate.

## Brand Personality

Calm, precise, dependable. A quiet instrument panel: numbers lead, chrome recedes, nothing moves unless something changed. Voice: short, plain, factual sentences; confident enough to be boring.

## Anti-references

- Carrier portals: cramped tables, rainbow charts, tiny fonts, blue-on-blue clutter.
- AI slop: glass cards, gradient blobs, gradient text, emoji icons, marketing voice inside the tool.
- Neon cyberpunk dashboards: black backgrounds with neon green, terminal cosplay.
- Enterprise admin consoles: gray-on-gray density with 8px text and everything a table.

## Design Principles

1. Two-second glance: GB left and pace status are the largest, first-read elements; everything else supports them.
2. One number, one truth: wherever a number is shown, the owner can correct it in place (plan size, GB left, calibration).
3. Earned familiarity: standard product vocabulary (side nav, forms, tables, buttons) in the states users expect; no invented affordances.
4. Quiet until it matters: neutral at rest; color, sound and motion are reserved for state change (warnings, cuts, saves).
5. Local and honest: everything stays on this machine; copy never promises what the product does not do.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- prefers-reduced-motion honored (existing support, keep it).
- Text and state colors target WCAG AA on the dark surface; status is never encoded by color alone (labels and icons carry the same meaning).
- Visible keyboard focus on every control (existing focus-visible behavior).
- Tabular numerals for all data; system font stack chosen for legibility.
